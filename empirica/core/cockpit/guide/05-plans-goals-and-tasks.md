# 5. Plans, goals and tasks

## Three sizes of work

- A **task** is one step: "write the welcome email".
- A **goal** is one result made of a few tasks: "welcome new clients properly", with the
  email, the checklist and the calendar invite as its tasks.
- A **plan** is the order in which goals and tasks get done, and what each depends on.

The practitioner keeps goals and tasks in the practice's memory, so they survive when you
close the cockpit, and any practitioner in that practice can pick them up.

## When to ask for a plan

Ask for a plan when the work has more than two or three steps, touches several documents or people, or
will take more than one sitting. Say: "Plan this first and show me before you start."
A good plan names each goal, its tasks, what has to be true for each to count as done, and
what it needs from you.

Small, single-step requests do not need a plan. Just ask.

## Why goals keep the work honest

Each task is closed with **evidence**: a link, a document, a sent email, a saved file. This
means "done" is something you can check, not just a feeling. When the practitioner reports,
each line points to the goal or task it belongs to, so you can see at a glance what moved.

## Asking about progress

- "What goals are open?" lists what is in progress.
- "What is waiting on me?" lists the decisions blocking the work.
- "Close the goals that are done." tidies up.

If a goal no longer matters, say so; the practitioner closes it with a note instead of
letting it linger.
