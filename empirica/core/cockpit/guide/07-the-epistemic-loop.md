# 7. Why it pauses to check

## Read before you write

The most useful habit a practitioner has is also the simplest: **look first, then act.**
Before it changes anything, it checks what is actually there, instead of acting on what it
assumes is there. Empirica builds that habit in. You will see it as short pauses where the
practitioner states what it knows before it acts.

## The three moments

Every piece of work runs through three moments. The names appear in the conversation, so
it helps to know them:

| Moment | What the practitioner does | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| **PREFLIGHT** | states the job, what it already knows and what it does not | "here is where I'm starting from" |
| **CHECK** | before changing anything, names the facts its next steps rely on, and how it knows each | "here is why I'm confident enough to act" |
| **POSTFLIGHT** | records what it learned, what it decided, what it got wrong | "here is what this taught me" |

Small, simple jobs skip the CHECK when the practitioner has already looked at everything
it needs. That is expected, not a shortcut.

## Why this matters to you

- **Fewer surprises.** It acts on what it saw, not on what it remembered or guessed.
- **A trail you can follow.** Every decision and finding is written down, so you or another
  practitioner can see later why something was done.
- **Honest confidence.** The practitioner rates how sure it is at each moment. Over time,
  Empirica compares those ratings with what actually happened, so the practitioner learns
  where it tends to be too sure or not sure enough.

## When it seems slow

If a pause feels unnecessary for a tiny request, say so: "this is a tiny edit, just do
it". For anything that touches several documents, a client, money or something hard to undo,
the pause is the point.
