# 9. Gardening: keeping its memory clean

## Why memory needs care

A practice remembers everything its practitioners record: findings, decisions, open
questions, goals. That is its strength. But memory ages. A finding that was true in March
may be false in June; a goal may have stopped mattering; a question may have been answered
and never marked as answered.

Old entries do not sit quietly. When the practitioner looks things up, they come back as if
they were current, and can steer it wrong. **Gardening** is the routine of tidying this up.

## What gardening does

- **Closes** goals that are done and questions that have been answered.
- **Marks as outdated** what was true once and no longer is.
- **Marks as wrong** what was never true. This is different from "outdated", and the
  difference matters: it is how the practice learns where it tends to make mistakes.
- **Removes** duplicates and test noise.

The practitioner proposes the changes and shows you the list before anything is removed.

## When to garden

- **When it surfaces something you know is old or wrong.** Say so; fixing that one entry
  takes a moment.
- **Every few weeks** for an active practice.
- **Before a release, a hand-over or a big new piece of work**, so it starts from a clean
  picture.

To start one, ask: "garden this practice", or type `/epistemic-gardening`.
